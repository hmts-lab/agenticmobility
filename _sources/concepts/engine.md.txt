# The engine

## A controlled Markov process

A run is a controlled Markov process in discrete time. The state `S_t` is everything in the world: where
every person and vehicle is and what it is doing, queues, prices, road conditions, the clock, and what every
agent remembers. In each step

1. exogenous input arrives: trip needs and scheduled events, all fixed by the scenario and the seed;
2. every agent that has a decision point is shown an observation, a function of the state alone and the same
   state for all of them, and its decision model returns an action from those its agency permits; an agent
   without authority there takes the default action;
3. the state moves on, with actions applied in a fixed order.

Neither the transition nor the observation has randomness of its own, so the next state depends only on the
current state, the actions and the exogenous input, never on how the state was reached. A decision model
needs no state of its own. This is why a recorded run can be replayed decision by decision to the identical
state, and why the same model can be put behind any agent.

## Decisions that take time

A kind's decisions may take time (`agents.<kind>.think_seconds`). The agent is shown the state as it is, and
its decision takes effect that much later, whoever decides: a rule, an algorithm or a language model. In
between the world moves on and the agent carries on as it was. When the decision takes effect it is carried
over to the state as it is by then; if it no longer applies (the car has passed the turning, the bus has
come) it is dropped as having come too late, and the agent is asked again if there is still something to
decide. A traveller who has a say starts deciding early enough to have decided when the trip is due.

Two things follow. A rule and a language model are compared like for like, both taking the same time. And
because an answer is not needed before the decision takes effect, the calls to a language model made within
one thinking time run side by side instead of each holding up the clock. On the testbed a traveller takes two
minutes over how to travel, a car half a minute over its route, a charging station and the orchestrator a
minute.

## Traffic

Roads are queues (`network.traffic: queue`). Every road has an exit capacity, the vehicles per hour and lane
that can leave it, and a storage, the vehicles that fit on it bumper to bumper. A vehicle drives along a road
at the free speed, then waits at its end, first come first served, until the exit has capacity for it and the
next road has room. A full road lets nobody in, so its queue grows back onto the road behind it; room that
opens at the front of a full road reaches its entrance only once the gap has travelled back along the queue.
A junction with traffic lights lets a road out only while its approach has green, at the saturation flow, and
a change of green costs a few seconds. An incident or bad weather cuts what a road lets out as it cuts its
speed. Each simulated car stands for several real ones (`network.capacity_scale`).

This is the link transmission model applied to single vehicles, the theory behind MATSim's queue model and
UXsim. Given the same network, vehicles, departure times and routes it reproduces the theoretical delay of
elementary cases within 3% and UXsim's mean trip times on the testbed's network within 1% to 6%
(`experiments/uxsim_check.py`). The earlier model, in which a road slows with the vehicles on it and never
fills, remains as `network.traffic: density`.

## Checking a run

`agmsim check` runs a scenario and holds it against a fixed list: every trip has one outcome, vehicles are
conserved on every road, no car is faster than free flow, no road holds more than fits on it or lets through
more than its capacity, the same seed gives the same run, and with more travellers or less road car trips do
not get quicker. For the testbed the list ends with the survey numbers it is tuned to, see
[](../testbed.md).

## Reproducibility

- **Seeded streams.** Demand, personas and car ownership depend only on the seed, never on policies, so
  experimental arms that share a seed face identical travellers.
- **Trace.** Every decision is recorded with what the agent saw, what it was permitted to do and what it
  chose; every tick stores a fingerprint of the full state.
- **Replay.** `agmsim replay runs/<name>` re-executes a run from its recorded decisions and verifies the state
  after every tick. A run driven by a language model is thereby reproduced exactly, without the model.
- **Cassette.** `llm.cassette: file.jsonl` records model responses; re-running with it makes no model calls.

Sampling temperature is not a reliable route to reproducibility, which is why AgMSim relies on recorded
decisions.
